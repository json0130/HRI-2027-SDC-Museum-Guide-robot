# Research questions for steps 5 and 6 (museum robot)

Questions only. No answers, no citations: nothing here has been looked up yet. The code for steps 5-6 stays marked as untuned placeholders until these are answered.

Step 5 = greeting wording and the first choice question. Step 6 = collecting information and building the profile.

## Step 5: the first move

1. Which first choice question works in a 1-2 hour guided visit, for adults and for children, and how should it be worded?
2. Does the wording need to differ by group composition (single, couple, family with children), and if so on what evidence about the group rather than a guess about relationships?
3. What do real museum-guide and tour-robot deployments report about opening behaviour (approach, greeting, first utterance, first question)?
4. How much does the opening matter for the rest of the visit compared with what happens later?
5. Is a wrong opening (for example child-directed wording to an adult) recoverable, and how quickly?

## Step 6: learning preferences

6. Explicit questions versus implicit cues for learning preferences: what is known about the cost of each in short, one-off interactions?
7. How many profiling questions do visitors tolerate before it feels like an interrogation?
8. Which preference dimensions can actually be elicited reliably (talk level, depth, pace), and by what signals?
9. Are those dimensions the right ones for a guide robot at all, or do other dimensions (for example proactivity, topic interest, language, accessibility needs) matter more?
10. What is the measured cost of a wrong personalisation versus a generic default?
11. How should evidence from different sources (an explicit answer, a spoken statement, a behavioural cue) be weighed, and is a fixed weighting defensible?
12. How fast do visitor preferences drift within one visit, and should old evidence decay?

## Children and groups

13. Children: consent, ethics and appropriate questioning of minors by a robot (who consents, what may be asked, what may be stored, what may be inferred).
14. Groups: who answers when a group is asked, and how do you stop one voice setting the profile for everyone?
15. Should a group have one profile, per-person profiles, or something else, and who decides when members disagree?

## Evaluation

16. What would a credible low-risk evaluation of steps 5-6 look like (measures, sample, setting) given that a simulator built on my own model of visitors says nothing about real ones?
17. Which outcome measures matter to museum staff and visitors (engagement, learning, comfort, dwell time, satisfaction), and which can be observed without storing anything about the visitor?
